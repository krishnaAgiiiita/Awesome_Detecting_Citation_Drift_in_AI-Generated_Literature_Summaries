# Curated Research Papers

The papers below were selected for relevance to citation drift, citation integrity, scientific summarization, factuality, retrieval-grounded generation, attribution, and verification. Links point to DOI, publisher, ACL Anthology, or arXiv records rather than redistributing copyrighted PDFs.

## Foundations and Citation Distortion

1. **How citation distortions create unfounded authority: analysis of a citation network**  
   Greenberg, S. A. (2009), *BMJ*.  
   [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.b2680)  
   Establishes how repeated citation can transform unsupported claims into apparently authoritative facts.

2. **Accuracy of cited “facts” in medical research articles: A review of study methodology and recalculation of quotation error rate**  
   Mogull, S. A. (2017), *PLoS ONE*.  
   [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0184727)  
   Provides a methodological baseline for measuring quotation and citation errors.

3. **Scientific citations favor positive results: a systematic review and meta-analysis**  
   Duyx, B., Urlings, M. J. E., Swaen, G., Bouter, L., & Zeegers, M. (2017), *Journal of Clinical Epidemiology*.  
   [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclinepi.2017.06.002)  
   Shows how citation patterns can systematically amplify positive findings.

4. **Temporal characteristics of retracted articles**  
   Bar-Ilan, J., & Halevi, G. (2018), *Scientometrics*.  
   [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11192-018-2802-y)  
   Useful for understanding how problematic claims can persist in citation networks after retraction.

## LLM Citation Reliability and Scientific Summarization

5. **Fabrication and errors in the bibliographic citations generated by ChatGPT**  
   Walters, W. H., & Wilder, E. I. (2023), *Scientific Reports*.  
   [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-023-41032-5)  
   Empirically measures fabricated and erroneous references generated by ChatGPT.

6. **ChatGPT and Bard exhibit spontaneous citation fabrication during psychiatry literature search**  
   McGowan, A., et al. (2023), *Psychiatry Research*.  
   [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.psychres.2023.115334)  
   Demonstrates severe citation-fabrication problems in a domain-specific literature-search setting.

7. **Evaluation of Large Language Model Performance and Reliability for Citations and References in Scholarly Writing: Cross-Disciplinary Study**  
   Mugaanyi, J., Cai, L., Cheng, S., Lu, C., & Huang, J. (2024), *Journal of Medical Internet Research*.  
   [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2196/52935)  
   Compares citation and DOI reliability across natural-science and humanities topics.

8. **Hallucination Rates and Reference Accuracy of ChatGPT and Bard for Systematic Reviews: Comparative Analysis**  
   Chelli, M., et al. (2024), *Journal of Medical Internet Research*.  
   [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2196/53164)  
   Quantifies hallucination, precision, recall, and reference accuracy in systematic-review prompting.

9. **Factuality challenges in the era of large language models and opportunities for fact-checking**  
   Augenstein, I., et al. (2024), *Nature Machine Intelligence*.  
   [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-024-00881-z)  
   Provides a broad technical framework for LLM factuality failures and fact-checking responses.

10. **Generalization bias in large language model summarization of scientific research**  
    Peters, U., & Chin-Yee, B. (2025), *Royal Society Open Science*.  
    [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.241776)  
    Shows that LLM summaries can broaden scientific conclusions beyond the evidence in source studies.

11. **Evaluating Large Language Models on Medical Evidence Summarization**  
    Tang, L., et al. (2023), *medRxiv*.  
    [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1101/2023.04.22.23288967)  
    Relevant to factual consistency and evidence preservation in high-stakes scientific summarization.

12. **Factuality of Large Language Models: A Survey**  
    Wang, Y., et al. (2024), *EMNLP 2024*.  
    [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.1088/)  
    Surveys factuality problems, evaluation methods, and mitigation strategies for LLMs.

## Citation Attribution and Verification

13. **Automatic Evaluation of Attribution by Large Language Models**  
    Yue, X., Wang, B., Zhang, K., Chen, Z., Su, Y., & Sun, H. (2023).  
    [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.06311)  
    Formalizes attribution errors and investigates automatic evaluation of whether generated claims are supported by cited sources.

14. **Assessing citation integrity in biomedical publications: corpus annotation and NLP models**  
    Sarol, M. J., Ming, S., Radhakrishna, S., Schneider, J., & Kilicoglu, H. (2024), *Bioinformatics*.  
    [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btae420)  
    Introduces annotated citation-context/evidence data and NLP models for citation-integrity assessment.

15. **CiteME: Can Language Models Accurately Cite Scientific Claims?**  
    Press, O., Hochlehnert, A., Prabhu, A., Udandarao, V., Press, O., & Bethge, M. (2024), NeurIPS Datasets and Benchmarks.  
    [Project](https://www.citeme.ai/)  
    Provides an attribution benchmark in which an excerpt must be mapped to the paper it actually cites.

16. **CiteAudit: You Cited It, But Did You Read It? A Benchmark for Verifying Scientific References in the LLM Era**  
    Yuan, Z., Shi, K., Zhang, Z., Sun, L., Chawla, N. V., & Ye, Y. (2026).  
    [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23452)  
    Frames citation verification as claim extraction, evidence retrieval, passage matching, reasoning, and calibrated judgment.

17. **CiteCheck: Retrieval-Grounded Detection of LLM Citation Hallucinations in Scientific Text**  
    Khajavi, K., Sadeghi, S., Adhikari, R., & Tessier, A. (2026).  
    [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27700)  
    Combines scholarly retrieval and structured verification to distinguish exact, minor, and major citation drift.

18. **CiteGuard: Faithful Citation Attribution for LLMs via Retrieval-Augmented Validation**  
    Choi, Y. M., Guo, X., Fung, Y. R., & Wang, Q. (2025/2026).  
    [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.17853)  
    Treats citation evaluation as retrieval-aware citation-attribution alignment.

## Retrieval-Grounded Generation and Citation Generation

19. **Enabling Large Language Models to Generate Text with Citations**  
    Gao, T., Yen, H., Yu, J., & Chen, D. (2023), *EMNLP 2023*.  
    [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14627)  
    Introduces ALCE, a benchmark for end-to-end answer generation with retrieval and citation evaluation.

20. **Self-RAG: Learning to Retrieve, Generate, and Critique through Self-Reflection**  
    Asai, A., Wu, Z., Wang, Y., Sil, A., & Hajishirzi, H. (2024), *ICLR 2024*.  
    [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11511)  
    Uses adaptive retrieval and reflection tokens to improve factuality and citation accuracy.

21. **Effective Large Language Model Adaptation for Improved Grounding and Citation Generation**  
    Ye, X., Sun, R., Arik, S. Ö., & Pfister, T. (2023).  
    [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09533)  
    Introduces AGREE for self-grounding and iterative citation improvement.

22. **In-Context Retrieval-Augmented Language Models**  
    Ram, O., Levine, Y., Dalmedigos, I., Muhlgay, D., Shashua, A., Leyton-Brown, K., & Shoham, Y. (2023), *TACL*.  
    [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1162/tacl_a_00605)  
    Demonstrates a lightweight retrieval-grounding approach that leaves the language-model architecture unchanged.

## Benchmarks and Scientific Text Processing

23. **CiteBench: A Benchmark for Scientific Citation Text Generation**  
    Funkquist, M., Kuznetsov, I., Hou, Y., & Gurevych, I. (2023), *EMNLP 2023*.  
    [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.455/)  
    Benchmarks scientific citation-text generation and supports systematic comparison of citation-generation systems.

24. **ALiiCE: Evaluating Positional Fine-grained Citation Generation**  
    Xu, Y., Gao, J., Yu, X., Bi, B., Shen, H., & Cheng, X. (2025), *NAACL 2025*.  
    [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-long.23/)  
    Focuses on fine-grained, positional citation generation rather than coarse document-level attribution.

25. **Long-form factuality in large language models**  
    Wei, J., et al. (2024).  
    [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.18802)  
    Introduces LongFact and SAFE for decomposing and evaluating long-form factual claims.

26. **Automatic summarization of scientific articles: A survey**  
    Altmami, N., & Menai, M. E. B. (2022), *Journal of King Saud University Computer and Information Sciences*.  
    [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jksuci.2020.04.020)  
    Provides background on scientific summarization and its structural differences from generic summarization.
